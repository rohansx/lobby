EmailWhen
Confirm your email for OrbitSomeone signs up and double opt-in is on.
Your place on the Orbit waitlistSomeone already on the list signs up again (at most every 10 minutes).
You're in: your Orbit inviteYou invite them.
Your broadcastYou send one.
New signup on OrbitTo you, if Notify me of new signups is set: the address, the page they came from, and who referred them.

All of them have a plain-text part and a simple HTML layout that renders in Gmail, Apple Mail and Outlook.

Sender name and reply-to

In Settings, From name sets the name people see (the project name by default) and Reply-to sends replies to your inbox. Replies to a waitlist email are often your first user feedback, so it's worth setting.

On Lobby Cloud, mail is sent from mail.trylobby.app. Sending from your own domain on Cloud is on the roadmap.

Unsubscribes and bounces

Every email to a signup has an unsubscribe link, and broadcasts also carry List-Unsubscribe headers for one-click unsubscribe. Unsubscribed people stay on the list but get no more broadcasts.

Self-hosting with Resend? Point a Resend webhook for bounces and complaints at /v1/hooks/resend and set RESEND_WEBHOOK_SECRET. Addresses that bounce or complain are then suppressed automatically.

Deliverability when self-hosting

A waitlist is only worth something if its emails land in the inbox. Before launch:

  1. Use a sending subdomain, such as mail.example.com, so waitlist mail doesn't affect your main domain's reputation.
  2. Add the SPF and DKIM records your provider gives you for that subdomain, and wait until it shows them as verified.
  3. Add a DMARC record. A monitoring policy is enough to start: _dmarc.example.com TXT "v=DMARC1; p=none".
  4. Test it: sign up from a fresh Gmail or Outlook address and check the confirmation arrives in the inbox, not spam.

Without an email provider configured, a self-hosted Lobby writes emails to its log instead of sending them. That's fine for trying it out, but nobody will be able to confirm.
